SHALLOW MINE WORKINGS AND FAULTS - DRILLING AND GROUTING: PROJECT DATA (WHOLE SITE)
Anonymised records from a coalfield site
================================================================================

1. WHAT IS IN THIS FOLDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
drilling_logs.xlsx
    The drilling logs for all 9,778 boreholes on the site that have both a log
    and a surveyed position (an area about 565 m east-west by 490 m
    north-south): 9,245 production holes, 504 perimeter holes, 26 test holes
    and 3 test-grout holes. They are as the site staff typed them: one
    worksheet per plot (plots 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, C, B, A, from south west
    to north east), with each borehole one block of the paper log form, one
    block after another. In each sheet the production and test holes come
    first, by line and column, and the perimeter holes of that plot follow.

    Each sheet has 11 columns. The first three are index columns, added when
    the records were typed up; they are not part of the printed form and carry
    no information of their own:
        A  line letter of the hole
        B  column number of the hole
           (A and B together are simply the hole number, e.g. G + 23 = G23,
            repeated on every row of that hole's block so that a sheet can be
            filtered or sorted by hole; for a perimeter hole A is P or PP and
            B its number, e.g. P + 135 = P135; for a test hole B carries the
            suffix, e.g. B + 8-T = B8-T, C + 55TG = C55TG)
        C  the line number within the printed form, running about 4 to 46
    The remaining eight columns are the form itself:
        D  description of strata        H  casing depth
        E  rig, and some heading text   I  bit size (mm)
        F  depth from ('gl' = ground    J  comments, and the value of the
           level); also holds the          BH No. and DATE DRILLED boxes
           "DRILLING LOG" heading       K  plot number
        G  depth to base of stratum

borehole_locations.csv
    Position of every one of those boreholes:
        hole_id         the hole's full name: plot + hole number, e.g. 4G23
        plot            plot number, as in the PLOT box of the log
        hole_no         hole number, as in the BH No. box of the log
        hole_type       production, perimeter, test (cored after grouting to
                        check the treated ground) or test grout (TG)
        x_m, y_m        local coordinates in metres; x increases east,
                        y increases north, from an arbitrary local origin just
                        south west of the site (x 5 to 569 m, y 13 to 504 m)
        ground_level_m  ground surface at the hole, in metres above a local
                        datum (the lowest hole in this set = 0.00)
    Every hole in the logs has a position and every position has a log; join on
    hole_id (plot + BH No.).
    A test hole was set out at a grid point, so it has the same position as the
    production hole of that number (e.g. 4B8-T and 4B8); it was in fact drilled
    close beside it.

faults.csv
    The faults in the worked seam, traced from the colliery's abandonment plan
    of that seam, in the same local x, y as the boreholes:
        fault_id        F1 ... F13 (numbered west to east)
        vertex          order of the points along the fault line
        x_m, y_m        local coordinates (m)
    Each fault is a polyline (join the vertices in order). The traces are
    clipped to the area of the boreholes plus 20 m, so the faults carry on
    beyond it.

fault_throws.csv
    One row per fault:
        throw_as_marked   the figure written beside the fault on the plan
        throw_ft          the same figure in decimal feet
        figure_uncertain  TRUE where the figure is hard to read or it is not
                          clear which line it belongs to
        length_in_area_m  length of the trace inside the area
        remarks           how faults connect, and anything odd about the figure
    The figure is the THROW: the vertical displacement of the seam between the
    two sides of the fault, in feet and inches as written on old British mine
    plans. 4/6 means 4 ft 6 in (1.37 m); 3/- means 3 ft 0 in (0.91 m).
    The plan does not say here which side is downthrown - that is for you to
    find from the boreholes.

read_drilling_logs.py
    Reads drilling_logs.xlsx and writes one row per soil/rock layer to
    strata_layers.csv (hole number, plot, driller, rig, layer description,
    depth from, depth to, thickness, casing, bit size, comments).
    Needs pandas and openpyxl.

plot_borehole_layout.py
    Draws the boreholes in plan, coloured by plot, with the fault traces and
    their throws. Needs pandas and matplotlib.

2. THE SITE AND THE GROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The ground is undermined by old, shallow coal workings. Before the land could be
built on, it was drilled on a close grid and the workings were filled with
grout. These are the drilling records from that work.

The sequence in nearly every log is:
  * Overburden / boulder clay (glacial till) from ground level down to rockhead,
    typically 8.5 to 20 m (median about 11.5 m);
  * Coal Measures bedrock below: mostly mudstone, some sandstone, and thin coal
    seams.
Holes are mostly 16 to 36 m deep (median 25 m); a very few are deeper or
shallower.

The logs describe the ground as it was drilled, so the words carry meaning:
  "Coal"                  intact coal was drilled (a pillar, or unworked coal)
  "Void"                  an open cavity - an unfilled working
  "Broken" / "soft"       disturbed or weak ground, typically collapsed workings
                          ("goaf"), or weathering near a subcrop
  "Grout"                 grout from an earlier hole was drilled through
  FWR / FAR               full water return / full air return (nothing lost)
  "Loss of flush"         the flush water or air escaped into the ground, which
                          usually means an open void or very broken ground

On the whole site: about 6,750 coal layers in about 5,540 holes, about 210
layers logged as void, 1,860 as broken, 5,970 as soft, and about 670 holes
drilled through earlier grout (counts by simple word search of the
descriptions). Coal was met mostly between 11 and 33 m depth.

3. THE COAL SEAMS AND THE FAULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three shallow seams underlie or subcrop in the area of the works. Their names
are withheld with the rest of the site information; they are called the upper,
worked and lower seam here. The strata dip gently to the north east (about
1 in 18 on the colliery plan).

  Upper seam
      Crosses the east of the site from north west to south east and subcrops
      there. Up to about 0.76 m thick.

  Worked seam  - the one that was mined, and the target of the grouting
      Subcrops along the southern boundary of the site and underlies most of it
      at depths between about 15 m and 60 m; about 0.55 to 1.14 m thick.
      Three conditions were found when drilling:
        - unworked coal: full seam thickness, no voids;
        - old workings with the coal pillars still standing: coal, and voids
          where the roadways are still open;
        - goaf, where the pillars were taken on retreat and the ground has
          collapsed: little or no coal, and much broken or soft ground.

  Lower seam
      Underlies the whole site at about 15 m to 70 m depth, in two leaves: the
      upper up to about 1.22 m and the lower up to about 0.92 m thick. It was
      not treated, but the deeper holes reached it.

Within one borehole the order of the seams cannot be mistaken, but which seams
a hole has reached depends on where it is and how deep it went. A hole that
stopped at 22 m has disproved nothing below 22 m, and a hole that drilled
through the worked horizon without logging coal may be telling you the coal was
worked away there, not that the seam is absent.

Faults. Thirteen fault traces cross the area. The throws written on the plan
are 1 ft 6 in (F2, F6), 2 ft 6 in (F3), 3 ft (F5, F7, F9) and 60 ft (F10);
the figures for F2, F5, F6, F7 and F10 are uncertain (see fault_throws.csv).
F10 is one of two parallel lines either side of a 60/- figure with an arrow,
which may together form one fault zone. The other faults have no figure that
can be read. Larger faults lie north and east of the area and are not included.

Things to think about:
  * across a fault the same seam sits at a different level: a smooth surface
    fitted through the coal picks will blur the step;
  * a fault on the plan is where the miners met it in the seam, so its trace
    is only as good as the workings were close;
  * a fault's throw can change along its length or die out;
  * a hole near a fault may meet broken ground, or the seam twice, or not at
    all.

4. HOW THE HOLES WERE SET OUT, AND THEIR SPACING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Local grid
  Holes were set out on a local grid of 60 m wide "plots" running north west to
  south east. Plots 1 to 8 are numbered upwards towards the south west; plots
  C, B and A lie north east of plot 1, in the north of the site.
  Each plot is divided along its length into "lines", lettered A-H, J-N, Q-Y
  (I, O and P are not used) at 3 m intervals, counted from the plot boundary
  towards the north east.
  Lines are divided at right angles into 3 m "columns", numbered 1, 2, 3... from
  north west to south east. Columns north west of column 1 are numbered 0, 999,
  998 and so on.
  A hole is named plot + line letter + column number: 4G23 is plot 4, line G,
  column 23. In the logs the plot is usually in its own cell, so the hole number
  alone may read "G23".

Drilling sequence and spacing
  Probe holes      first, at 12 m spacing by line and 48 m by column number, to
                   give advance notice of the seam depth.
  Primary infill   two overlapping 6 m x 6 m square grids aligned with the local
                   grid; the second grid is offset so each of its holes sits at
                   the centre of four holes of the first. (Where the worked seam
                   is intact, only one grid was used.)
  Secondary infill drilled only around a probe or primary hole that took more
                   than 15 tonnes of grout: four holes 3 m from it, forming a
                   square turned 45 degrees to the local grid.
  Perimeter holes  along the site boundaries, numbered P1, P2 ... and (a later
                   campaign) PP1, PP2 .... Included where the hole has a
                   surveyed position (504 holes); about 230 perimeter holes,
                   mostly in plot 1, have a log but no position and are left
                   out.
  Test holes       drilled after grouting to check the treated ground (named
                   with -T), and test-grout holes (TG). Included where the
                   hole has a position (29 holes); a few without one are left
                   out.

Because the local grid runs north west to south east, any straight section line
in x or y cuts across the plots at an angle, so the holes do not form straight
lines along it. Deciding how to project them onto a section is part of the
work.

5. DATA QUALITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The logs were typed on site by the supervising staff and have not been
corrected. They contain the usual errors: layers whose depths overlap or leave a
gap, descriptions missing from a layer, misspellings, a form or two typed
slightly out of place, and blank or impossible values here and there (one hole
has a depth of over 200 m, for example). Finding them and deciding what to do
about them is part of the exercise.
The fault traces were digitised from a scanned plan; positions are good to a
few metres, and the throw figures are as legible as the scan allows.
